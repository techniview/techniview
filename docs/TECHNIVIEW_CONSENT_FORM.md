**Pilot Dates:** February 15 to March 14, 2027

**Who may participate:** You must be 18 years of age or older when you sign this form. If you are under 18, do not sign this form or participate in the pilot.

## About the Pilot

TechniView is a coding practice platform. This research pilot asks whether using the platform helps students practice technical interview problems and where the platform needs improvement. The pilot lasts four weeks. You can use the platform at your own pace; there is no required number of problems or hours.

You may complete assigned coding problems and explore other problems. The study team will use your activity and survey responses to evaluate usability, coding performance, and self-reported confidence. Results may be used in research, presentations, or published reports.

Taking part is voluntary. You may decline or withdraw at any time without penalty. Your decision will not affect your course grade or required coursework. See "Your Choices and Questions" for what happens to data already collected.

---

## What You Will Do

You will create a TechniView account and may work on coding problems during the pilot.

The team will offer one one-hour usability session each week for four weeks in Rhodes or Baldwin Building on the UC campus. You may attend any number of sessions, including none. At a session, you will use TechniView and report issues you encounter. If you attend, you will be asked to complete a short Qualtrics survey before and after that session. You will also be asked to complete a Qualtrics survey before and after the four-week pilot. These surveys ask about your confidence, interview readiness, coding practice outside TechniView, ease of use, and feedback on TechniView. Survey time is in addition to any time you spend using the platform.

---

## Information Collected During the Pilot

The study team may collect and use the following information associated with your account and pilot activity:

- **Account information:** your name, email address, account role, account creation time, and email-verification status. TechniView uses an internal account ID to associate activity with your account. We do not collect a student ID for this pilot.
- **Problem activity:** the time you first open a problem, submission and completion times, attempts and retries, pass status, score, and error categories such as wrong answers, compilation errors, runtime errors, timeouts, and resource-limit errors.
- **Submitted work and execution results:** the source code you submit, including practice submissions; whether a run passes or fails; execution time and memory use; and program output, error output, or compiler output. TechniView stores submitted code and execution results.
- **AI complexity estimates:** for completed submissions, TechniView sends your source code and the problem title and statement to an AI service on a UC-operated GPU host to estimate time and space complexity. The estimates are stored with your submission and shown as feedback. They do not affect grading, and the study does not use them as research outcomes.
- **Account and service security information:** account session records, email-verification and password-reset records, and source-address checks used to limit repeated signup, join, and account-recovery requests.
- **Survey answers:** UC-hosted Qualtrics collects your name and email address with your pre- and post-pilot survey answers and, if you attend a usability session, your pre- and post-session survey answers. The study team can associate those answers with you.
- **Bug reports:** if you report a bug through GitHub issues, the team will review the report and whether it can reproduce the problem. Your GitHub username and report may be visible to others, depending on the repository's settings.

---

## How We Use the Information

We use this information to provide the coding practice service, show you your progress and execution results, operate and secure TechniView, troubleshoot problems, and evaluate usability, coding performance, and self-reported confidence during the pilot. Pilot results may be used in research, presentations, or published reports.

---

## Risks and Possible Benefits

You may feel frustrated or embarrassed while solving problems or answering questions about your performance. There is also a risk that someone could gain unauthorized access to information that identifies you or reveals your activity or survey answers. The team limits access to authorized researchers and uses password-protected, encrypted systems. If you post a GitHub issue, other people may be able to see your GitHub username and what you wrote.

You may find the coding practice useful, but there is no guaranteed benefit to you. The findings may help improve TechniView. You can choose not to take part in the research.

---

## Food and Gift Cards

Food will be available to participants who attend a usability session. At the end of the pilot, the team will award one $25 gift card to the participant who successfully completes the most distinct problems and one $25 gift card to the participant who reports the most unique software bugs that the team can reproduce. Bug reports must be submitted through GitHub issues. Requests for a feature or a change in UI preference do not count as bugs. If participants tie for an award, the winner will be the person who first reached that final count, based on the time of their last qualifying problem completion or GitHub issue submission. An award will be made only if at least one qualifying completion or bug report exists in that category. You do not need to attend every session to be eligible. If you withdraw, eligible activity and bug reports completed before withdrawal will still count. The team will email the gift card as a digital code to the winner within 30 days after the pilot ends. No partial gift cards will be awarded. Food is available at each session you attend, even if you later withdraw.

---

## Who Can Access the Information

You can see your own progress and submission results. Members of the TechniView pilot team who have completed the training required by the University of Cincinnati’s applicable IRB process may access identifiable information to operate, maintain, secure, troubleshoot, and evaluate the platform. Other participants cannot see your individual results through TechniView.

TechniView is hosted on systems operated within the University of Cincinnati. Resend processes account-related email. UC-hosted Qualtrics hosts the pilot surveys. The TechniView code-execution service runs submitted code, and the AI service on a UC-operated GPU host processes completed submissions for complexity feedback.

---

## Research Data and Sharing

We plan to share grouped summaries and a de-identified research dataset. The dataset will not include names, email addresses, submitted source code, or raw program output. It will include repeated pseudonymous codes for participants, problems, and assignment context, along with submission status, scores, error categories, late-work indicators, and relative timing information.

Repeated participant codes let us connect records from the same person. The codes will not show your name or email, but activity patterns may still make someone recognizable. Before sharing the dataset, we will remove the link between participant codes and account identities.

Other researchers may use the shared de-identified dataset in future research without contacting you for additional consent, if UC requirements allow it.

---

## Storage and Retention

TechniView stores account, activity, submission, and execution information on systems operated within UC. At the end of the pilot, we will prepare the de-identified research dataset in accordance with applicable UC IRB requirements. Submitted code and raw program output will not be included in the shared dataset. Research records will be kept for at least five years after the study ends under UC retention requirements. The retention or deletion of the original code, output, and other identifiable information will follow applicable UC requirements. The de-identified dataset may be kept indefinitely if those requirements allow.

---

## Your Choices and Questions

You may withdraw at any time by contacting Derek Corniello at corniedj@mail.uc.edu. Your pilot access will end, and TechniView will stop collecting new activity data about you. Data already collected will not be removed. We will retain survey responses and data already included in grouped summaries. Other existing pilot data will be de-identified at the end of the pilot, in accordance with applicable UC IRB requirements.

For questions about the pilot or this form, or to request a summary of the results after the pilot, contact Derek Corniello at corniedj@mail.uc.edu. For questions about your rights as a research participant, contact the UC Human Research Protection Program/IRB at (513) 558-5259 or irb@ucmail.uc.edu.
